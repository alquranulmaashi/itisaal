One Verb, Fourteen Endings

In Arabic the past-tense verb carries its subject inside it. Keep the stem, change the ending, and the verb tells you who did it. You'll practise on a dozen sound three-letter verbs, then see the same endings in the Qur'an.

الجذع — stem النهاية — ending
Which verbs?

الفِعْلُ الصَّحِيحُ السَّالِمSound three-letter verbs

These verbs are the easiest to conjugate because the stem never bends. A verb is “sound” when its three root letters are all plain consonants:

No weak letterno و، ا، ي in the root
No hamzano ء in the root
No doublingno repeated letter like مدّ

The big idea

جِذْعٌ + نِهَايَةStem + ending = who did it

Take كَتَبَ (he wrote). Split off the ending and you can swap it for any pronoun.

كَتَبْتُ = “I wrote.” No separate word for “I” is needed — the ending already says it. With the endings that start with ت or ن, the last root letter takes a sukūn first.

!
Two endings change when a pronoun attaches: ـُوا loses its silent alif (كَتَبُوا → كَتَبُوهَا), and ـْتُمْ becomes ـْتُمُو (كَتَبْتُمُوهَا).
From the Qur’an
وَمَا قَتَلُوهُ وَمَا صَلَبُوهُ
Sūrah an-Nisā’, 4:157 — “they did not kill him, nor crucify him.” The alif of ـُوا drops before ـهُ.
لَوْلَا إِذْ سَمِعْتُمُوهُ ظَنَّ الْمُؤْمِنُونَ
Sūrah an-Nūr, 24:12 — “why, when you heard it, did the believers not think well…” Here ـْتُمْ becomes ـْتُمُو.
وَلَقَدْ نَصَرَكُمُ اللَّهُ بِبَدْرٍ
Sūrah Āl ‘Imrān, 3:123 — “Allah had already given you victory at Badr.”
حَمَلَتْهُ أُمُّهُ كُرْهًا
Sūrah al-Aḥqāf, 46:15 — “his mother carried him with hardship.”
In sentences

الجُمَلVerb first, or subject first

With the verb first, it stays singular and only matches gender. With the subject first, the verb matches in gender and number.

Verb first
كَتَبَ إِبْرَاهِيمُ رِسَالَةًIbrahim wrote a letter
كَتَبَتْ مَرْيَمُ رِسَالَةًMaryam wrote a letter
Subject first — the verb agrees fully
إِبْرَاهِيمُ كَتَبَ رِسَالَةًhe — singular
مَرْيَمُ كَتَبَتْ رِسَالَةًshe — singular
إِبْرَاهِيمُ وَإِسْمَاعِيلُ كَتَبَا الرَّسَائِلَtwo men — dual
مَرْيَمُ وَزَيْنَبُ كَتَبَتَا رَسَائِلَtwo women — dual
الأَوْلَادُ كَتَبُوا رِسَالَةًthe boys — plural
النِّسَاءُ كَتَبْنَ رَسَائِلَthe women — plural

verb subject
From the Qur’an — verb first stays singular
وَدَخَلَ مَعَهُ السِّجْنَ فَتَيَانِ
Sūrah Yūsuf, 12:36 — “and two young men entered the prison with him.” The verb دَخَلَ stays singular even though the subject فَتَيَانِ is dual.

The endings, person by person

وَذَا النُّونِ إِذ ذَّهَبَ مُغَاضِبًا
he — ـَ · Sūrah al-Anbiyā’, 21:87: “and [remember] Dhun-Nun, when he went off in anger.”
فَلَمَّا سَمِعَتْ بِمَكْرِهِنَّ أَرْسَلَتْ إِلَيْهِنَّ
she — ـَتْ · Sūrah Yūsuf, 12:31: “when she heard of their scheming, she sent for them.”
فَلَمَّا ذَهَبُوا بِهِ وَأَجْمَعُوا أَن يَجْعَلُوهُ فِي غَيَابَتِ الْجُبِّ
they (m.) — ـُوا · Sūrah Yūsuf, 12:15: “so when they took him away and agreed to put him into the well.”
رَبِّ إِنِّي ظَلَمْتُ نَفْسِي فَاغْفِرْ لِي
I — ـْتُ · Sūrah al-Qaṣaṣ, 28:16: “My Lord, I have wronged myself, so forgive me.”
رَبَّنَا مَا خَلَقْتَ هَٰذَا بَاطِلًا
you (m. sing.) — ـْتَ · Sūrah Āl ‘Imrān, 3:191: “Our Lord, You did not create this in vain.”
أَكَفَرْتُم بَعْدَ إِيمَانِكُمْ
you (m. pl.) — ـْتُمْ · Sūrah Āl ‘Imrān, 3:106: “Did you disbelieve after your belief?”
إِنَّا فَتَحْنَا لَكَ فَتْحًا مُّبِينًا
we — ـْنَا · Sūrah al-Fatḥ, 48:1: “Indeed, We have granted you a clear conquest.”
